# Lore

> He is not haunting your world. He is trying to get back into it, and you
> are the one holding the door open.

---

## The short version

Two brothers built a home. The younger one went too deep, and something older
than the world came back up wearing him. It killed the family. The elder
brother could not kill his own brother, so he did the only other thing — he
tore a hole and put him through it, and sealed it.

That was a long time ago and it was not your world. But it is the same
*place*, and the seal is not a door. It is a scar. Scars thin.

**What thins it is you.** Every time you dig deeper, take more, build bigger,
you do exactly what the younger brother did — and the thing on the other side
feels the shape of it, recognises it, and pushes.

---

## Fixing the pitch

Your version has the right bones. Three changes make it carry a whole game.

**1. The player stops being a bystander.**

In your version this is Steve's story and you are watching it. Move the player
into the centre: he does not come for you because you are there, he comes for
you because **you are repeating his brother's mistake**. Digging. Hoarding.
Going deeper than you need to. He sees his brother in you.

That makes Wrath diegetic. It is not a difficulty counter — it is how much you
have started to look like the person he became.

**2. The early signs are warnings, not threats.**

This is the change that makes the whole thing re-read on a second playthrough.

`go back` · `stop digging` · `it is not your house` · `this is deep enough`

Played straight, those are a monster taunting you. But if the brother is still
in there — buried under the thing that took him — then for the first several
hours **he is trying to save you**, in the only language he has left, and you
read it as a threat and dig faster.

By the time he is hunting you, it is because the last of him is gone. The
player never gets told this. They work it out, or they do not.

**3. He copies you because he is trying to remember himself.**

Mimicry with a motive is far worse than mimicry as a gimmick. He wears your
skin and your name not to trick you — to *try it on*. He has forgotten what
being a person was. Finding him standing in your base wearing your face,
facing the wall, doing nothing, is the single most upsetting image available
to this mod, and it costs almost nothing to build.

## What actually happened

Never stated outright. This is for us, so the details stay consistent.

- Two brothers, a valley, a house. Ordinary.
- The younger dug. Not for ore — he had enough. He dug because it was there.
- Below the deepest stone is something that predates generation. Not a mob.
  Not a god. Closer to a *rule* the world was built over the top of. It has
  no shape, so it takes one.
- It came up wearing him. It did not pretend well at first. There was a
  stretch where he was still partly himself, and knew, and could not stop.
- It killed the family with his hands.
- The elder brother could not put an axe in his brother's chest. So he opened
  something instead, and pushed him through, and closed it.
- The elder brother is long dead. Nobody remembers either of them. The name
  on the signs has been scratched out for so long that the scratching is the
  only part still legible.

## The reveal schedule

Horror is the order you learn things in. This is the actual design.

| Phase | What the player believes |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Nothing. Maybe a bug. Maybe they misremembered. |
| 2 | Something is in the world with them. It is hostile. |
| 3 | It was a person once. It knows their name. |
| 4 | It is not one thing — something is wearing something else. |
| 5 | It is coming *through*, and their own progress is what let it. |
| End | The signs at the start were warnings. |

The last row is the whole point. If a player finishes and goes back to their
first screenshot of `go back` and feels sick, the mod worked.

## The dimension

Never visited. The player never goes there, and there is no portal to build.
That is deliberate — a place you can visit becomes a level, and a level is not
frightening. It only ever **leaks**.

The leak is the visual language:

- **Violet** is the thing. Corruption in his skin, the fissures, the light
  under a sealed cave room, the tint in the rain.
- **White** is what is left of him. Only his eyes. Nothing else.

Two glow colours, two meanings, and the balance shifts across the phases: at
phase 1 the eyes dominate; by phase 5 the violet has spread over almost all of
him and the white is nearly gone. **The player watches the brother disappear
without ever being told that is what they are watching.**

## The ending

The Effigy binds him to a body. You can hurt him. You win, and he dies.

Then one more sign, where the fight was, in a voice that is not the one that
has been writing them:

```
thank you
```

Killing him did not kill the thing that took him. It let go of him. It is
still down there, and the next person to dig too deep is a different save
file.

## Voice — for sign text

Rules that keep the writing from turning into a horror-game cliché generator:

- **lowercase, no full stops.** Punctuation reads as composed. He is not.
- **Short.** Four words beats ten. Sign width is a gift, not a limit.
- **Present tense, second person.** `you left the door open`, not `someone
  opened your door`.
- **Never explain, never threaten explicitly.** No "I will kill you". The
  threat is that he knows things.
- **Specificity is the weapon.** Generic dread is free and worthless. `i can
  see the light` is fine. Your bed coordinates are devastating.

**Phase 2 — warnings that read as threats**
```
go back          stop digging        this is deep enough
not deeper       it is awake         it is not your house
```

**Phase 3 — he knows you**
```
<player name>         do you sleep         you left the door open
i can see the light   i was here first
```

**Phase 4–5 — nothing left of him**
```
<bed coordinates>     you are the same      i remember hands
open it               almost
```

## What we never explain

- What it is.
- Why it was under the world.
- Whether the elder brother was right.
- What the scratched-out name was — **except once**, on one sign, in one
  world, at the very end.

Leaving these alone is not laziness. Every answer is smaller than the
question, and the player's own guess will be better than ours.
